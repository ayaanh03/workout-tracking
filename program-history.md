## 2026-08-07 — Three-run week; Sat becomes lift-only; Thu + Sun permanent off (v4.4; §1, §2.A–§2.C, §3.B, §4.C–§4.E, §6, §11.E, §12; Adj #244)

Athlete directive: **run 3× per week maximum, Thursday and Sunday permanent off days, strictly one long / one tempo / one VO2max per week until race day.** Effective **W17 Mon 2026-08-10** — W16 runs to completion under v4.3 by explicit athlete instruction ("leave this week alone").

**What changed.** The Monday Z1 easy run is deleted and the long run takes its slot; Saturday keeps Hypertrophy B and loses its run. The resulting week is **Mon long / Tue Hyp A / Wed threshold / Thu off / Fri VO2max / Sat Hyp B / Sun off** — 3 runs, 2 lifts, 2 full rest days. Both quality sessions keep their existing days, so the Wed→Fri 48 h spacing, every §10 test placement (W19 cut-down Wed Aug 26, W23 tune-up Fri Sep 25), and the entire pace-arbitration chain (Adj #202/#222/#232/#241) carry over untouched. Lifting is completely unaffected — same two circuits, same days, same doses, same progression (§5).

**Why the long went to Monday rather than Friday or Wednesday.** Three constraints decided it. (a) **Seated calf is Saturday** (§3.B) and the L Achilles is reactive (Adj #34/#113/#211/#230) — putting a quality run on Monday would land hard running ~40 h after the calf sweep; the long is Z1 and is the mildest session that can sit there. (b) **Adherence.** The long has been the failure point of the last three weeks — unperformed W14 Sun (Adj #214) and W15 Sun (Adj #233), and displaced again in W16 (Adj #243). Monday off a full Sunday rest, first session of the week, is the highest-adherence slot available; the Saturday/Sunday slot has demonstrably not worked. (c) Keeping T on Wed and I on Fri preserves 48 h between the two quality sessions and 72 h from Fri's VO2 back to Mon's long.

**Costs, recorded honestly.** Peak mileage falls **28 → ~20–21 mpw** (~25%); the v4.3 28-mpw ceiling (Adj #67) stops being binding. The easy fraction falls to **~70–75% by time / ~65% by distance** — outer edge of polarized, below the textbook 80% — and the Mon long is now the *entire* easy fraction, so a skipped Monday leaves a week of nothing but hard running. There is no punt slot left: Sunday is off by rule. Aerobic development over the remaining 11 weeks slows accordingly. Against that: sub-21:30 is a 6:55/mi target, not an elite-mileage one; the quality work — which is what drives 5K performance at this level — is untouched; and the shape is essentially the FIRST/Furman three-quality-runs model, which has a real evidence base at 5K through marathon. Cutting one weight-bearing session per week also removes ~20% of impact exposure with two reactive tendons (L Achilles, R patellar) and an SI history on file.

**Downstream re-pointings.** The §2.A ≥6 h AM-run/PM-lift gap is void (Saturday has no run) and the "push the lift to Sunday" fallback dies with it — a missed Saturday lift is simply missed. Hill strides move from the Mon easy run to **post-Mon-long** (4–6 reps when the long is ≥65 min; treadmill 4–6% still an accepted substitute) — standard post-long stride placement, and now the week's only lower-body neuromuscular dose (§6), so reps get cut before the slot does. **The AM Achilles read for any gated calf bump moves from Sat AM to Mon AM**, the first run after the calf sweep. Taper (§2.C) holds three runs through W26; W27 race week is race-anchored and carries three pre-race runs plus the Saturday race, with Thu and Sun still off. W23's long is trimmed 60 → 50 min and drops strides — there is no longer an easy day to absorb a hard Monday four days out from the tune-up.

**Not programmed, available:** Saturday is now run-free, so a Z1 row (45–60 min, 2:15–2:25/500m, RPE 4–5) can be added there to restore aerobic volume with zero impact and without adding a run. Precedent: the W16 Mon row (Adj #234/#235), which the athlete directed and which ran clean.

## 2026-08-10 — Hill strides removed from the program (v4.5; §0, §1, §2.A–§2.C, §4.D–§4.E, §6, §7.B, §9, §11.A–§11.C, §12; Adj #255)

Athlete directive, three days into v4.4: **"I want us to simply remove these strides."** Effective immediately — the W17 Monday slot was the last one that ever appeared in a brief.

**What changed.** Every stride prescription is out: the Monday post-long block (6–8 × 10–12 s @ 4–6% grade, walk-down, 4–6 reps when the long is ≥65 min), the flat 4–8 × ~20 s fallback that covered an unavailable grade, all `(+ hill strides)` entries in the §4.D Build and §4.E Sharpen week tables, the §2.C taper lower-body NM row, the §11.A Phase B advance rung, the §11.B "held during deload" clause, the §11.C yellow-day "skip hill strides if Mon" instruction, the §9 phase-gated allowance and the §7.B eligibility rule. The §4.B and §4.C tables keep their stride entries because those phases are complete and the tables are a historical record; both headers now say so.

**What survives.** Race-week priming strides only — the W27 Friday shakeout (4 × ~20 s) and the race-day warmup (4 × 100m, §4.F). Those are activation before a maximal effort, not the Phase B+ neuromuscular training dose that was removed. If the athlete wants those gone too, that is a taper-time decision.

**Why this is mostly bookkeeping.** The hill-specific dose had not been performed since **W10 Mon 2026-06-22** — nine weeks and five consecutive slots: W13 consumed by the twice-displaced 5K TT (Adj #167), W14 skipped (Adj #194), W15 substituted flat because no grade was available (Adj #215), W16 skipped after the row swap (Adj #236), W17 skipped today (Adj #254). Each miss generated a carry-forward flag, a "next slot" pointer, and a line in the morning brief; none of that produced a rep. A prescription that has gone unexecuted for nine weeks while consuming attention every week is not a prescription, and deleting it costs nothing that was actually being banked.

**The cost, stated plainly.** §6's lower-body durability/RE row now has **no neuromuscular entry at all**. The soleus wall-sit was retired at W7 (2026-06-02, athlete directive) and hill strides were explicitly named at that time as the remaining Phase B+ lower-body NM work; with both gone, nothing in the program supplies concentric/elastic lower-limb loading. This matters more under v4.4 than it would have under v4.3, because a three-run week already carries less varied mechanical stimulus than a four-run week. The cited benefit of consistent hill strides is roughly **1–2% running economy** — but "when consistent" is doing the work in that sentence, and nine weeks of non-compliance means the realized loss from this deletion is close to zero. What is genuinely forfeited is the *option* on that 1–2%, not the 1–2% itself.

**What remains available if RE stalls.** The §3.C opt-in adds — B-stance hip thrust (light), reverse Nordic, Spanish squat — are untouched, low-shear, SI-compatible, and none has been taken. They sit in the Saturday lift slot rather than requiring a run-day appendage, which is a better fit for an athlete who has now skipped the run-appended version five times running. If economy plateaus in Sharpen, that is the re-entry point, and it should be a deliberate one-at-a-time addition with the standard 48-hour test, not a quiet restoration of strides.

**Not changed.** Mileage, both quality sessions, all §10 test placements, the T/I pace-arbitration chain (Adj #202/#222/#232/#241/#251), the entire lift side, and every open load gate. The Monday long is unaffected in duration or intent — it simply ends when it ends.

## 2026-08-12 — Threshold band stepped 7:45–7:50 → 7:40–7:45 (athlete directive; Adj #271)

**What changed.** §4.A's threshold row moves from **7:45–7:50/mi to 7:40–7:45/mi**, and the row-substitution split from 1:54–1:59 to **1:52–1:57**/500m. **The RPE ≤7 anchor is unchanged and still governs** — it is the thing that decides whether the new band is right, exactly as it was for the old one. Easy, M, I, R and goal race pace are all untouched; this is a threshold-only step.

**Why now.** The athlete's call after the W17 Wed session (*"we should go faster next T run"*), and the session gave it a foundation rather than a hunch. The 6×1K ran the prescribed 7:50 slow-end open on **28:49 of work — the longest threshold dose of the Build** — and returned **RPE 7 flat across all six reps** with *"mega easy"* attached, at a 7:44/mi average. That was the honest test the Adj #241 arm had been waiting for, and it came back on the wrong side of the trigger: the reopen it armed pointed toward *slower* (7:50–7:55) on a firm over-anchor read, and instead the band's slow edge proved comfortable at full dose (Adj #267).

**Why this size of step, and not a bigger one.** The 5 s/mi is not a guess — it is a reserve that two prior recalibrations deliberately withheld. Adj #166 (W12) and Adj #202 (W14) both set T off the *raw* time-trial VDOT while explicitly noting the heat-adjusted figure implied a faster band and holding it back: the W14 3K TT read **VDOT 42.85 raw / ~43.5 heat-adjusted**, and Adj #202 recorded "raw-implied exactly 7:45, heat-adjusted ~7:40 in reserve — same conservative posture as Adj #166." Both trials were run in 80–85°F. **7:40–7:45 is that reserve released, not new territory** — it is where the athlete's own tested fitness has sat since 7-22, discounted for heat that has been paid for twice over in RPE evidence since. Going further (7:35 or faster) would be past every measurement on record, and the next session it applies to is **W18 Wed's 4×1 mi @ T** — longer continuous reps with 90 s rest, where a mis-set band compounds across the rep instead of being absorbed by a 60 s recovery, the way today's fast 7:35 opener was. The conservative posture is kept, just at the correct anchor.

**How this reads against the standing "no recalc outside tests" rule (Adj #202).** That posture is not overturned — it was written to stop a single cheap session from moving a band, and it is still the reason the **I** band did not move after three consecutive sub-anchor reads (Adj #232/#251), one of which was doubly confounded (Adj #252). Two things separate this from that case: the input here is a full-dose session run *at* the prescription rather than a short or deviated one, and **the athlete is directing the change**, which is a different kind of input than a data reading. The rule holds for everything else — **I stays 7:00–7:15 pending Friday's 5×1000m** (Adj #251), and the **W19 cut-down test (Wed 8-26) remains the arbiter** for both bands. If the cut-down says otherwise, the cut-down wins.

**Registered read at the new band.** W18 Wed, 4×1 mi @ 7:40–7:45, 90 s rest, opened **on pace, not fast** (Adj #268 — the fast open is now a three-session habit and a mile rep forgives it less). **RPE ≤7 across the four → the step is correct and holds into the W19 test. A firm over-anchor read — RPE 8+, or a rep that dies the way W15's second 15-minute rep did — reverts to 7:45–7:50 immediately**, and that revert is not a failure of the athlete's call, it is the anchor doing its job. Cadence and the tendon watches are unaffected by pace band per se, but note the mechanical direction: **a faster band means a longer stride at the same turnover**, and cadence has read sub-band outdoors at every intensity measured (Adj #269), so Friday's and next Wednesday's cadence readings carry slightly more weight than they did.

**Not changed.** Easy/long 9:15–10:30 (conversation-gated), M 8:15–8:25, I 7:00–7:15, R 6:35–6:45, goal race pace 6:55. The §10 test schedule, the weekly template (v4.4/v4.5), mileage, and the entire lift side are untouched.

## v4.6 — 2026-08-22: cable pushdown switched to a two-handed rope (athlete directive, Adj #303)

**What changed.** The triceps movement in both lift circuits — §3.A Tue (4×12) and §3.B Sat (4×12–15) — changes from **unilateral cable pushdown to a two-handed rope pushdown**. **Why.** Athlete directive at logging on 2026-08-22: *"switch this to both handed with rope from here on out, the form is way cleaner."* Mechanics reported by the athlete are the whole basis, and they are worth taking seriously here rather than treating as preference — a movement being run with cleaner form is a tendon-relevant input in a program whose triceps prescription is shaped entirely by an elbow (§10.A).

**What it reverses, and the watch that comes with it.** The unilateral spec is not arbitrary: it dates to **2026-06-09**, when bilateral attachments were found to aggravate the lateral elbow, and it has stood since. **This change reintroduces exactly the variable that was removed.** What makes that reasonable now and did not in June: the elbow has run **15 consecutive clean sessions**, the **§10.A four-week symptom-free gate has been MET since 7-31** (Adj #229) with the eligibility banked, and the tendon has been silent through every load step and every dose collapse of the Build. What does not follow is that the June finding was wrong. **Registered accordingly: any lateral-elbow symptom at the rope reverts to unilateral on the spot — no waiting for a pattern, no negotiation.** The §8 tennis-elbow rules are preserved verbatim and unchanged; this sits underneath them, not against them.

**What it does to the load record.** 55 lb on the stack with both hands is nominally the same ~27.5/arm the unilateral version was running, but the rope changes grip, wrist angle and stabilization, and the movement is no longer performed one side at a time. **The load line therefore restarts at a new baseline** — the first rope outing (W18 Sat: 55×10/8/8/7, all sets to failure, 33 total) is not read as the fourth point on the unilateral series, even though it points the same direction as the three collapses that preceded it (Adj #288/#304).

**Open question this does not settle.** The triceps **dose** question stands where Adj #288/#304 left it: 27.5/arm would not support four working sets in either slot, and the first rope outing did not hold the 12-rep floor on any set. **W19 Tue 8-25 is a load-or-set-count call on the rope** — drop the stack to 45–50 for a four-set dose, or hold 55 for three sets. The §10.A second biceps/triceps movement stays off the table (Adj #266/#288); the next clean window for that expansion is W21 Tue 9-1, since W20 is a deload.
