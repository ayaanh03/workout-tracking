# Default recovery and the next workout

**Standing athlete directive, November 4, 2026 (America/New_York), decision #471:** assume recovery is good and generate the next workout whenever a session is logged. Policy ID: `2026-11-04-assume-good-recovery-and-auto-next`. The athlete will report a rough morning or other change so the plan can be adjusted.

## Ordinary recovery

- Use **assumed good recovery — athlete default** for ordinary workout generation, run classification/progression, cycling tolerance and the recovery component of a completed normal ramp-week review. Do not require a routine recovery answer or hold earned progression solely because one is absent.
- Keep the assumption separate from observed facts. Unreported next-day recovery, symptoms and metrics remain null; record the policy and decision supporting the assumption instead of inventing a report. It may resolve currently pending ordinary decisions, but cannot revive previously cancelled credit or overwrite completed prescriptions.
- Actual performance is still required: completed work, reps/RIR or pace/RPE, comparable conditions, progression limits and a completed week where applicable. The assumption cannot complete future sessions or advance stages on calendar rollover.
- Reported poor recovery, illness, unusual fatigue, worsening symptoms or relevant performance decline overrides the default. Adjust the affected next workout promptly. Existing injury limits, unresolved return/fatigue restrictions and specific test or restoration requirements remain in force; assumed general recovery does not establish symptom resolution or clear them.

## Generate after logging

1. Reconcile the reported session and any corrections into state and history, retaining its exact original prescription and actuals.
2. Apply performance progression and the recovery default, then generate and publish the **next scheduled uncompleted workout** without a separate request. Include saved bike settings or the prescribed run's Watch file when applicable.
3. Date the next workout honestly. If it is in the future, label it a preview; retain the actual local calendar date in live state and keep future completion statuses uncompleted. Show completed sessions in history and the week view.
4. A duplicate log or correction updates the existing session and its next-workout decision; it does not add another exposure, skip another day or generate a chain of unperformed sessions. A backfilled older log must not replace a later valid upcoming workout with an obsolete date.

This is the assistant's standing workflow, not a background scheduler or browser decision engine. A later recovery update can revise the issued workout; do not silently rewrite a performed prescription.
